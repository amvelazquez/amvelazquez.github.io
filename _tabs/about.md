Welcome! I study international and comparative political economy, with a regional focus on Latin America. Broadly, I examine who has the authority to shape a society's place in the global economy and how citizens, firms, and governments respond when that authority is contested. My research investigates how globalization and international shocks redistribute political authority, and how struggles over sovereignty and legitimacy shape the politics of foreign investment, trade, and territorial control while leaving lasting consequences for political development and state-society relations.

To answer these questions, I combine survey experiments, geospatial methods, text analysis, formal theory, and fieldwork. Across projects, I investigate how international economic and geopolitical pressures reshape political authority, public opinion, and institutional development.

I am currently a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science at Washington University in St. Louis.
